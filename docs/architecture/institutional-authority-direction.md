# Institutional Authority Direction

The long-term authority question is:

> Can this person perform this action, for this Client, under this Engagement, at this risk level, right now—and why?

Potential resolution inputs are Actor, Role, Partner Status, office-based or personal authority, Firm Delegation, Client Mandate, Engagement, system and environment, Risk Classification, policy, and required approvals.

```text
Client Mandate
      ∩
Firm Authority
      ∩
Specific Approval
      ∩
Current conditions and controls
      =
Permitted Action
```

Technical access proves none of these gates. The Responsible Partner remains the root of professional accountability for an Engagement but need not approve every action. Security Authority may approve privileged access, Change Authority may approve a high-risk Change, and an Engineer may execute it. Each function must be explicit.

## Personal and office authority

Managing Partner authority attaches to the office and transfers with its holder. A Founding Partner Reserved Matter right attaches to constitutional Partner status and may be personal. Neither should be copied into ad hoc permissions on a person's profile.

## Risk

Risk Classification, Risk Owner, Risk Acceptance, Control, Residual Risk, and Policy Exception are emerging operational concepts. Future design must answer who may accept a particular risk for the Firm or Client. It should not import enterprise-risk bureaucracy without an operational need.

This is an architectural direction, not authorization for a generalized policy engine.
