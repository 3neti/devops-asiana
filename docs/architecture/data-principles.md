# Data Principles

## Historical integrity

Important institutional states are versioned or recorded as transitions. Finalized history is not overwritten to resemble the present.

## Explicit state

Approval, execution, verification, and closure are separately recorded. Critical state is not inferred from a neighboring action.

## Traceability

Material action identifies actor, time, authority, reason, subject, and source. Human and system actors are distinguishable.

## Evidence linkage

Domain records link to an evidence index; sensitive payloads may live in controlled external storage with ownership, custody, integrity, and retention metadata.

## Proposal and acceptance

Where risk warrants, lifecycle transitions preserve Proposed, Reviewed, Approved, Executed, Verified, and Closed events. Rejection, withdrawal, expiry, cancellation, and failure remain visible.

## No silent exception

A deviation points to an approved Policy Exception. Free-text explanations do not confer authority.

## Ownership and authority

Client ownership of infrastructure, data, or credentials is separately represented from Firm custody and delegated operational authority.

## Relational first

Use intentional relational constraints and transactions for actual invariants. Append-only audit concepts are sufficient; no blockchain mechanism is proposed.
