## Principle

> **No Incident Without Disclosure.**

The Firm shall identify, manage, communicate, learn from, and preserve evidence of Incidents without blame-oriented concealment.

## Classification and roles

An event is an observed occurrence; an alert signals a condition requiring evaluation; an Incident is an unplanned degradation, interruption, security concern, or material operational risk. A Major Incident requires coordinated command because of severity, breadth, duration, sensitivity, or stakeholder impact. A Security Incident involves suspected compromise of confidentiality, integrity, availability, or authorized control.

Each declared Incident shall have severity, Incident Commander, Responsible Partner, Technical Lead, communication owner, affected Client and Engagement, timeline, and current status. The Incident Commander coordinates response; the Responsible Partner retains professional and Client accountability.

Response shall address declaration, safety, containment, evidence preservation, investigation, recovery, verification, Client notification, and legal or regulatory escalation where required. Communications shall be timely, candid, accurate, authorized, and updated as facts change. No person shall conceal an Incident to protect reputation or metrics.

Major and qualifying Incidents require a blameless post-incident review covering impact, timeline, contributing conditions, decisions, control performance, recovery, communications, and corrective actions. Corrective actions require owners, due dates, evidence, and closure review.
