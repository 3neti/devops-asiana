# Law-Firm and SRE Model

The law-firm model contributes client acceptance, conflicts discipline, engagement letters, a single accountable Partner, controlled authority, professional judgment, confidentiality, and institutional continuity.

The SRE model contributes least privilege, observable systems, reversible change, incident command, blameless learning, continuity testing, explicit service objectives, and durable operational evidence.

Together they require:

- an accepted Client before work begins;
- an approved Engagement defining scope and responsibility;
- exactly one Responsible Partner for each material Engagement;
- authority grants rather than status-based access;
- recorded, risk-classified changes with recovery plans;
- disclosed incidents with owned communications and corrective action;
- proof that approvals and controls occurred.

Professional judgment may alter the route through an emergency, but it never makes the event invisible. Expedited action must be recorded and reviewed.

## Why the law-firm analogy matters

The Firm is not retained as a pool of technical manpower, and the Client does not retain an AI agent. AI and automation may perform substantial technical work; the professional institution supervises, authorizes, reviews, verifies, and stands behind that work within a defined Engagement. This is the accountability relationship that makes the law-firm analogy substantive rather than cosmetic.

The doctrine is:

> AI can perform the work. The Firm accepts responsibility for the work.

That doctrine does not create unlimited liability. It preserves the boundaries between Client Mandate, Firm Authority, Specific Approval, Technical Access, and professional responsibility. Every material Engagement still requires one Responsible Partner, even when execution is delegated to people, software, automation, or AI agents.

As execution becomes cheaper and faster, verification, recovery, evidence, and professional judgment become more important than the ability to perform an isolated technical action. Evidence is therefore a professional asset, not merely an audit by-product.
