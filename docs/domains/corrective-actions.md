# Corrective Actions Domain

A Corrective Action is a durable institutional obligation to remedy a material finding or control gap. It is not a generic task, personal reminder, or project-management item.

## Source and independence

Every Corrective Action identifies an attributable source: an Incident, failed Change or post-implementation review, Break-glass review, Access Review, Policy Exception, or another evidenced finding. The source record and the Corrective Action have independent lifecycles. Closing an Incident or Change does not complete, verify, cancel, or close the remediation it produced.

```text
Source Finding → Assignment → Remediation → Completion Claim → Independent Verification → Closure
```

Each stage establishes a different fact. Execution does not imply completion; a completion claim does not imply verification; verification does not imply closure.

## Accountability

An accepted Corrective Action has exactly one accountable owner. A service team may perform work and contributors may record progress, but the institutional question “Who owns the outcome?” has one answer. Assignment records the assigner, authority basis, owner acceptance, time, and evidence.

The owner may claim completion against declared acceptance criteria. The owner may not independently verify that claim. A qualified verifier records the verification standard, observed result, outcome, time, and evidence. Competent authority then makes any closure decision separately.

## Time and escalation

A Corrective Action retains an append-only due-date history. The current due date is the latest valid entry; it does not overwrite prior commitments. Every initial date and later change identifies the actor, authority basis, reason, decision time, and evidence.

Overdue work remains visible. It requires an escalation record identifying the actor, authority, audience, reason, time, and evidence. Escalation does not silently extend the due date or accept the residual risk.

## Governing requirement and risk

Each action identifies the exact governing policy version, requirement, and control being remediated, plus risk classification, impact, risk owner, and residual risk if delayed. Authority and Delegation governs assignment, escalation, and closure authority. Source-specific policy governs the finding and remediation obligation.

Cancellation and supersession are explicit dispositions with authority, reason, time, and evidence. Neither may erase the original finding, assignment, history, or work record.
